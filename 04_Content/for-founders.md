## A home for the business you built.

You spent years turning something small into something real. You know your
customers by name, your product inside and out, and exactly which parts you
would never hand off. That is worth a lot. It is also worth protecting.

Fullsteam buys and grows software companies that serve one industry. We keep
your name on the door, your team in place, and your product pointed at the same
customers it already serves. Then we put a bigger company behind it.

## Your name stays. Your team stays.

This is the part worth being blunt about, because it is where most founders
worry.

**The brand stays yours.** We are not rebranding you into a portfolio. Your
customers do not get a new logo in the mail. The name they chose and the name
they trust is the name they keep.

**The team stays.** Your people know the product. They know the customers. They
know the weird edge cases nobody wrote down. We have no interest in making them
learn a new business. We keep the team, and we give them more to work with —
shared infrastructure, shared support, and a company that is bigger than any one
vertical on its own.

**The roadmap keeps moving.** A vertical software company grows by getting
better at one industry, not by becoming a different company. We keep it pointed
the same direction. What changes is how much is behind it.

## A conversation, then a close you can count on.

Nobody should sign their company away over a single meeting. Here is how the
process actually works.

### We talk

It starts as a conversation, not a pitch. Tell us what your business does, who
it serves, and what is working. Tell us what is not, too — the thing you have
been meaning to fix for two years, the market you have been meaning to enter.

You will get straight answers, including about what we are not looking for. We
would rather tell you early than waste three months of your time.

### We see if the fit is real

Both of us are looking for the same thing: that this is genuinely the right
home, for reasons that are about the business and not just the transaction.

We look hard at what you have built and what it needs next. You have lived in
the industry. You know whether we are reading it right, and we want to hear it
when we are not.

Sometimes the honest answer is that we are not the right owner, and we say so.
That conversation is worth more than a deal you regret.

### We close without a late surprise

This is the part founders tell us matters most, and it is a process problem,
not a promise.

No surprises arrive after signing. The diligence is done before anything is
papered, the numbers are looked at properly, and the terms are plain. By the
time you are signing, there should be nothing left to discover.

Certainty of close is not a side benefit here. It is the point.

## The business keeps going — here

The risk with selling a company is what happens next. Day one of a new owner
can look like a stranger learning your product in real time, in front of your
customers.

This is the same team, the same product, the same customers. Nothing about the
day-to-day changes shape because the ownership changed.

And there is a version of your business that is bigger than the one you built
alone. Fullsteam runs payments, lending, insurance, and AI inside the software
that vertical companies already use. Your customers get more from the software
they already rely on, without you having to build those pieces yourself.

You built for one industry. We make sure the company behind it keeps up.

## Software built for one industry.

Fullsteam has grown [[CONFIRM: number of businesses — V2 says "100+"; on the
§20.7 publishability list]] software businesses, and most of them were built the
same way: by someone who understood one industry better than anyone and decided
to build the software that industry was missing.

We keep buying the ones that are still good at that. Deep industry knowledge is
the asset. It is the whole reason the business works, and it does not get
replaced by us.

## Confirm who we are. Then ask for a conversation.

We will not pretend a website can answer everything. What we can do is tell you
plainly who you would be dealing with, and let you decide whether it is worth
talking.

If you are building software for a single industry, and you want to know what
happens next, start there.
