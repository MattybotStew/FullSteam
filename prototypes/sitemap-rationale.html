Why This Sitemap

The rationale behind every structural choice in sitemap.html. Each decision traces back to the client brief: reposition Fullsteam.com from a corporate brochure into a bold storytelling experience — without touching the brand identity.

← Sitemap diagram Header wireframe →

1 From brochure to story — organize, don't redesign the brand

The brief is explicit: the brand identity stays unchanged. What changes is how content and navigation are organized so visitors quickly understand Fullsteam through its portfolio of market-leading vertical software companies and embedded products.

  • Keep: logo, palette (Bio Blue / Gold / Green), voice.
  • Change: information architecture — the sitemap is a reorganization tool, not a rebrand artifact.
  • Feel: more exploratory than corporate — visitors should understand the company by moving through its solutions, not by reading an "About" wall first.
Mapped to sitemapAll nodes are dashed wireframe blocks with the brand palette applied only as accents — structure carries the meaning, not surface styling.

2 Two core stories drive the two mega-nav items stories

The site must tell two stories. In the sitemap they become the only two nav items that branch into children — everything else is a leaf.

Story (from brief)Nav itemChildren in the tree

3 Flat vertical list instead of "a page for every industry"

The brief states all four audiences should explore representative companies and verticals without requiring a page for every industry. The sitemap therefore uses a hub + detail pattern inside Solutions:

  • Flat list of all 11 verticals on the hub and in the Menu overlay — no macro-category labels (updated 2026-09-10; supersedes earlier category-chip grouping).
  • A dedicated Solutions hub page (/solutions) is the primary-CTA destination, with a find-your-vertical filter/type-ahead — the exploratory front door, not a nav dump.
  • All 11 verticals get a detail page from one reusable template (locked 2026-09-10) for SEO and future acquisitions; they are linked from the hub, not listed as 11 chrome nav items.
  • The homepage shows a revenue-ordered subset; the hub carries the full list.
Brief requirement"All four groups should understand the vision and explore representative companies and verticals without requiring a page for every industry."

4 Founder path first — investors served by proof, not a nav node audiences

Founders (software sellers) are the rank-1 audience that needs a single-purpose entrance, so For Founders is a leaf at the front of the rail. For Investors was removed from the sitemap (2026-09-10) — investors are a co-primary audience but are served through the homepage scale/proof story and footer paths rather than a dedicated nav branch.

  • For Founders is a single page, not a mega-menu: it is a conversion path, not a browsing path. The single-page badge marks it as a leaf.
  • Leftmost placement = highest-priority entrance, matching the header chrome where it leads the primary nav.
  • Investors get no L1 node; investor-facing proof (scale, growth, portfolio breadth, inferable KPIs) lives on the homepage and in the footer.
  • Rank-2 audiences (employees, customers) are served through Careers, Our Story, and Solutions/Offerings respectively.
Mapped to sitemapFor Founders is the first cell under the Home rail. There is no For Investors node.

5 A lean Home block

Home in the sitemap carries exactly three jobs: hero framing, the For Founders audience path (investors are served by scale/proof on the page — no L1 node), and a short brand-story summary. It then hands visitors off downward — visually represented by the single stroke to the rail — rather than trying to be the destination itself.

  • The fuller CTA phrase ("Explore our vertical solutions across hospitality, retail, wellness, field services, and more…") lives in hero copy while the button stays short (per header-wireframe note).
  • An exploratory site needs a light front door: heavy content lives one level down, at Solutions and Offerings.
Mapped to sitemapHome is the only solid-bordered node — it anchors the tree and stands apart from the dashed placeholder content below it.

6 The primary CTA pulls everything toward Solutions cta

The brief asks how to shift the primary CTA toward "Explore our vertical solutions." The sitemap answers structurally, not just with copy:

  • Solutions sits mid-rail in the nav row and gets the largest subtree — the center of gravity of the whole map.
  • The header CTA button shortens to "Explore Solutions"; the full phrase lives in hero and mega-menu footer copy (mirroring header-wireframe.html).
  • Contact remains a utility link, not a nav branch — CTAs lead to exploration first; contact is always available secondarily.
Brief question"How to shift the primary CTA toward 'Explore our vertical solutions'?"

8 Constraints & assumptions

  • CMS: Duda anticipated retained — the tree maps cleanly to Duda's page/group model (nav groups become menu items; category chips map to folder pages or anchor sections).
  • Leaf stubs: "single page" badges mean one template, no children — founders, investors, Our Story, Careers, plus utility legal pages noted at the foot of the sitemap.
  • Brand untouched: all styling is low-fi wireframe convention; palette appears only per the V.1 06/2018 spec.
  • Future-proofing: the Solutions grouping absorbs acquired verticals without nav surgery — expected given the acquire-and-grow mechanism.

9 Naming the embedded-expansion axis: why "Offerings"

The nav label for the second core story (Payments, Lending, Insurance, Hardware, Integrations, AI) was iterated through several alternatives before landing on "Offerings":

AlternativeAssessment
Platform (original)Client rejected — too abstract and tech-forward for a site that should feel exploratory rather than corporate.
CapabilitiesProfessional and flexible. Communicates what Fullsteam can do. Fits well alongside "Solutions." Slightly less familiar as a standalone nav label — visitors may need the sub-label to decode it on first glance.
ProductsDirect and clear. Used successfully by Square for the same dual-axis purpose. Risk: can feel generic if not paired with a strong supporting tagline.
Offerings selectedMatches the brief's own language ("embedded offerings"). Softer, more consultative tone. Distinctive without being obscure. Works for all four audiences — investors read it as a product line, founders as capabilities, customers as services, employees as what the company builds.
ServicesUniversally understood, but risks misdirecting visitors toward a service-desk or consulting page rather than a product stack.
How We GrowTells the Embedded Expansion story directly. Strong for investors and founders. Longer than ideal for a top-nav slot and less conventional.
ExpansionPulled directly from the "Embedded Expansion" story. Concise but may not immediately communicate payments and lending to a first-time visitor.
GrowthInvestor-friendly and short. Too vague on its own — needs the sub-pages and sub-label to do the explaining.
Decision"Offerings" was selected for its alignment with the brief's language, its consultative tone, and its ability to encompass both financial products (payments, lending, insurance) and technical ones (hardware, integrations, AI) under one clear label.
