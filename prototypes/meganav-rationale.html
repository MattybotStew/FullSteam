Why Two Mega-Menus — and Why They're Different

Solutions and Offerings are the only nav items that branch. Each mega-menu is shaped by the story it carries: one invites exploration of a portfolio, the other enumerates a product stack.

← Header wireframe Sitemap diagram → Sitemap rationale →

1 Mega-menus as the vehicle for the two core stories solutions offerings

The brief says the site must tell two stories and feel exploratory rather than corporate. Ordinary dropdowns hide content; a mega-menu exposes breadth at a glance. Since both stories are portfolio-wide, each gets a panel wide enough to show its whole shape in one hover:

  • Solutions mega (flat list) tells the Acquire & Grow story — the visitor sees all 11 verticals at once, instantly communicating "market-leading software companies across many industries."
  • Offerings mega (520px, two-column list) tells the Embedded Expansion story — a compact enumeration of five embedded products plus AI, reading like a product stack rather than a directory.
  • Different shapes for different jobs: Solutions is organized browse-first, Offerings is organized read-first.

2 Solutions mega: flat vertical list (updated 2026-09-10)

Supersedes earlier grouped-category rationale. Macro-category labels (Hospitality & Events, etc.) were removed. The Menu overlay and Solutions hub now show all 11 verticals in two columns plus find-your-vertical.

  • Self-identification over taxonomy: visitors scan industry names directly — Hospitality, Wine, ERP — without an intermediate category layer.
  • Find your vertical filter on the hub covers visitors who do not want to browse the list.
  • Descriptional micro-copy under every item ("management", "specialty POS", "lube, repair, lots") keeps jargon low for non-technical audiences.
Brief requirementAudiences should explore representative companies and verticals "without requiring a page for every industry" — the hub + overlay surfaces everything without 11 chrome nav items.

3 Offerings mega: a concrete product stack

The Offerings panel is deliberately narrower (520px) and uses a simple two-column ruled list: Payments, Lending, Insurance, Hardware, Integrations, AI at Fullsteam — each with its descriptor ("embedded processing", "embedded financing"…).

  • The repeated "embedded" prefix does the positioning work: it's the Embedded Expansion story compressed into five labels.
  • One product per line makes each service feel substantial — supporting the brief's ask to present embedded services through concrete examples; the products' detail lives on their own pages per the sitemap.
  • AI at Fullsteam is green-flagged as an innovation accent — the only colored list item. It answers the client's open question ("should AI merit a dedicated page?") with a visible dedicated entry, while keeping it grouped under Offerings because AI-enabled features ship through the embedded products.
Mapped to wireframeThe green AI row in header-wireframe.html matches the green-outlined AI node in sitemap.html and the green "AI highlight" note in both legends.

4 Both panels end in story-carrying footers

Every mega-menu footer carries a full-phrase link rather than a generic "more":

  • Solutions → "See how our Offerings grows our software →"
  • Offerings → "See how our Offerings grows our software →"

These double as the primary CTA surfaces: the brief wants the primary CTA shifted toward "Explore our vertical solutions," so exploration links sit at the bottom of every expansion path — including the hero ghost button and header CTA. The button text stays short in the chrome; the full phrase lives where there is room for storytelling.

Brief question"How to shift the primary CTA toward 'Explore our vertical solutions'?" — answered by making that phrase the persistent exit of every mega-menu.

5 Interaction model: low-friction discovery

  • Desktop: open on hover or keyboard focus (:hover / :focus-within) — zero clicks to see the breadth of the portfolio.
  • Touch/mobile: hamburger stacks the nav; tapping a caret item toggles its panel open inline, and panels collapse to a single column grid.
  • Panels overlay content with a soft shadow rather than pushing the page down — exploration stays anchored to the nav, reinforcing the "front door is light, depth is one level down" structure from the sitemap rationale (§5).
  • Dashed borders + brand-blue top rule keep the wireframe convention while signaling the panel belongs to the header system.
