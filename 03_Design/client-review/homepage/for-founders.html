“It wasn’t an exit — it was a home for the business we built.”

Founder questions, answered.

Do you only care about our payments?

Software comes first. Payments is how the business grows after that. We acquire systems of record for an industry, then expand with embedded payments, lending, and insurance when it fits the product.

Is this an exit?

No. Fullsteam is a home for the business you built — not a turn-and-burn exit. The brand stays, the team stays, and the product keeps its name.

What happens to our name and our team?

The business unit keeps its identity. People stay in their roles; growth comes from shared services behind the scenes, not from replacing the team you built.

How sure is the close?

Certainty of close is central to how we work. When we commit to a transaction, we intend to finish — founders should not plan around a deal that might fall through at the last mile.

Do you understand what we do?

We buy vertical software, not generic roll-ups. Diligence focuses on the industry, the customers, and the product — not on forcing the business into a one-size-fits-all platform.

When do our customers hear about this?

Not at close. Announcements are case by case, usually after the business has settled into the portfolio — not as a day-one press release.

What does Fullsteam actually buy?

System-of-record software built for one industry — the application a business runs on every day. Software is most of the story; payments and AI expand the model after the core product is right.

Does AI replace my team?

No. AI is used to make the software better and to take repetitive work off small teams. Use cases come first; it is never framed as staff reduction.
