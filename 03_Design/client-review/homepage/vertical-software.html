01 Vertical 01 / 11

Service runs on the software, not beside it.

Covers, turns, tabs, and tips move faster than a back office can keep up. Hospitality software has to hold the whole service in one place.

A restaurant's night is a sequence of commitments: the reservation book, the floor plan, the check, the tip pool, the close. Software that models only the point of sale leaves the rest of the shift in notebooks and group chats.

We build for the operating core — the part a general platform cannot reach without becoming a hospitality company. The brand, the team, and the way the house runs stay exactly where they are.

02 Vertical 02 / 11

Every event is a one-off, and every one has a date.

Weddings and events run on timelines that do not move and details that cannot be re-run. The software has to hold both.

A venue sells a date, then assembles a hundred small dependencies around it: vendors, layouts, payments, staff, weather plans. Most of that coordination still lives in spreadsheets until the week of.

Vertical software earns its place by owning that assembly — the proposal, the timeline, the deposit schedule — so the team is running the event instead of reconciling it.

03 Vertical 03 / 11

Vintage, allocation, and compliance in one ledger.

Wine moves through clubs, allocations, tasting rooms, and three tiers of regulation. One bottle has to be right in every one of them.

A winery's inventory is not a count; it is a set of commitments by vintage, club tier, and channel. Compliance adds a second ledger on top, and the two disagree at the worst possible moment.

Software built for the category keeps allocation, membership, and reporting on the same record — which is what lets a small team sell direct without losing the thread.

04 Vertical 04 / 11

The counter and the back office are one system.

Independent retail lives between the shop floor and the books. The software has to close that gap every day.

Pricing, purchasing, loyalty, and shrink all meet at the counter, usually in front of a customer. When the systems behind them disagree, the store absorbs the cost in time and trust.

We back software that treats inventory and margin as one problem, so an owner can see the business as it actually runs — not a month later in a spreadsheet.

05 Vertical 05 / 11

Recurring revenue with a physical constraint.

Units and slips are rented by the month and bounded by space. The software has to know both.

Occupancy, waitlists, gate access, and autopay are simple in a demo and stubborn in practice: seasonal demand, delinquencies, and a site manager who needs answers on a phone.

Vertical software makes the physical inventory legible — what is vacant, what is promised, what is late — and turns the monthly cycle into something that runs itself.

06 Vertical 06 / 11

Schedules, records, and billing that cannot drift.

A clinic runs on time it cannot get back. Software that drifts costs practitioners the hour and the claim.

Appointments, intake, treatment notes, and reimbursement are one continuous record in practice, and three disconnected systems in most software. The gap shows up as no-shows and denials.

We build for the clinical day as it happens — the calendar, the chart, the claim — so the front desk and the practitioner work from the same version of the truth.

07 Vertical 07 / 11

The job is finished in the field, not the office.

Dispatch is a promise about time. Field software has to keep it with bad signal and a full truck.

A technician needs the history, the parts, the checklist, and the invoice before leaving the driveway. The office needs the schedule to survive traffic, weather, and a job that runs long.

Software built for the trade closes the loop on site: the work order becomes the invoice, and the office sees the day as it is actually going.

Margin lives in the details of every load.

Dispatch, compliance, and settlement have to agree before the truck leaves. Usually they do not.

A carrier prices a load, assigns a driver, tracks hours, and settles pay against rules that change by state and by customer. Every handoff is a chance to lose margin quietly.

Category software keeps the load, the driver, and the paperwork on one record — so the numbers hold from quote to settlement.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

Do you buy software companies, or add services to them?

We buy software businesses. Services do not lead. Software is the system of record, and AI and payments are added on top of it — not in place of it.

Do you change the brand or the team?

No. The companies keep their names, their teams, and their relationship with their customers. What changes is what sits behind them: shared engineering, go-to-market support, and capital.

Is AI part of this strategy?

Yes, as an addition to software that already runs the business — not as the headline. AI is most useful where the system of record is already in place.
